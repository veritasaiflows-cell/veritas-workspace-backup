# Integrated Sector Diversification Recommendation - 2026-05-14

## Conclusion

The next diversification work should stay **research-first, not deployment-first**. The best immediate lane is **LLY / XLV Healthcare**, followed closely by a **Quality Staples lane led by WMT and KO**. None of the candidates are promotable or deployable from the current evidence alone.

The reason is straightforward: the portfolio is already at the direct Technology cap and has broader Tech + AI-power concentration, but the non-Tech sector tape is not strong enough to force capital into lagging areas. Use diversification research to prepare the bench, not to override current execution discipline.

## Why LLY stays first

LLY has the best combination of **business quality and portfolio construction value**:

- Healthcare is currently absent from the draft model portfolio.
- LLY offers non-Tech growth exposure instead of another AI / data-center / cloud-linked name.
- Recent company evidence supports strong business momentum: the Healthcare packet cites Q1 2026 revenue growth of 56%, non-GAAP EPS growth of 156%, and raised FY2026 guidance.
- The downside is not thesis quality; it is **entry quality and sector confirmation**.

Current blockers:

- LLY is watch-only / sector monitor in the Execution Board.
- Full Portfolio View shows LLY above its written band: close 1007.99 vs 907.64-969.88 band, stop 876.52.
- XLV is barely above its 50DMA and underperforming SPY over 1d / 5d / 20d.
- Valuation and GLP-1 concentration make chase discipline mandatory.

**LLY verdict:** preferred Healthcare monitor; do not promote, size, or deploy until pullback/base or sector repair supports a formal promotion-review packet.

## Why Staples is second, and which names matter

Staples is the right ballast lane to build after Healthcare because it directly offsets the portfolio's Tech/AI concentration and adds lower-correlation demand exposure. But the lane is mixed: the highest-quality names are expensive, and the cheaper defensive names have weaker charts.

Priority inside Staples:

1. **WMT** — best first deep dive. It has the strongest blend of operating momentum, omnichannel/e-commerce/advertising leverage, and chart strength. Main blocker: demanding valuation.
2. **KO** — best defensive ballast. Strong brand/pricing-power profile, low beta, high margins, and constructive chart. Main blocker: lower growth and still-not-cheap valuation.
3. **COST** — best business, not best stock at this price. Excellent compounding quality, but valuation is too rich to treat as a clean ballast entry.
4. **XLP** — best basket monitor if the goal is sector signal rather than single-name selection.
5. **PG / PEP** — keep as repair/value monitors until they reclaim moving averages.

**Staples verdict:** open as a research queue lane; WMT and KO deserve first single-name follow-up. No watchlist promotion from this packet alone.
