## Discovery Objective

Understand one HVAC missed-call recovery and callback-handoff workflow without requesting credentials, sensitive customer data, call recordings, private customer lists, texting workflows, emergency-routing control, or production system access.

## Opening Frame

I am looking at one narrow workflow: what happens when an inbound HVAC inquiry is missed or not booked, then assigned for callback and handoff-status tracking. The goal is to map visibility and intake clarity. This is not a revenue guarantee, booking guarantee, emergency-response system, or production automation setup.

## Qualification Questions

1. What type of HVAC work do you mostly handle: residential service, install/replacement, maintenance plans, commercial, or mixed?
2. How do most new requests arrive: phone, web form, chat, email, referral, home warranty, ads, or existing customers?
3. What system do you currently use for scheduling or dispatch: ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, spreadsheet, calendar, paper, or something else?
4. Who answers calls during business hours?
5. What happens when calls come in after hours or when the office is busy?
6. What counts as missed, unbooked, follow-up needed, not a fit, or callback assigned in your current process?
7. Where do requests most often get stuck: missed calls, voicemail, slow callback, incomplete information, callback ownership, or handoff-status tracking?
8. Do you have a written intake checklist or phone script?
9. What information does dispatch need before sending a technician?
10. What would make this workflow easier for your team without changing your production systems?

## Boundary Questions

1. Can this review use only redacted examples or synthetic examples?
2. Are there any customer details, recordings, phone numbers, addresses, or private notes that should be excluded?
3. Are calls recorded, transcribed, or texted back today? If yes, do not share samples until a separate privacy/legal boundary is approved.
4. Are there emergency, no-heat, no-cool, or safety-sensitive situations that require special disclaimers or human escalation?
5. What should this Sprint explicitly not touch?
