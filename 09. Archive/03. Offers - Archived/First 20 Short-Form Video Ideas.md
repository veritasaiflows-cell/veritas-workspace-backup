# First 20 Short-Form Video Ideas

> Archived note. This file belongs to a retired operating branch. Preserve it as historical context, not current doctrine. Current live identity and operating rules are defined by SOUL.md, IDENTITY.md, AGENTS.md, MEMORY.md, and FINANCE_SOUL.MD.

## Purpose

These are the first content ideas for the content-first confidence-building model.

Rule:
Lead with pain, then sharper truth, then one practical shift.

## Pillar 1. Too many ideas, not enough execution

### 1. You do not have a motivation problem
Hook:
You probably do not have a motivation problem. You have too many competing priorities.

### 2. Too many ideas is not always a strength
Hook:
Having lots of ideas sounds like a strength until none of them actually move.

### 3. Busy is not the same as clear
Hook:
A lot of ambitious people are not lazy. They are just directionally overloaded.

### 4. Why you keep revisiting the same ideas
Hook:
If you keep rethinking the same ideas, the problem is probably not discipline. It is lack of decision closure.

### 5. Idea sprawl kills execution
Hook:
One of the fastest ways to ruin execution is to keep every idea mentally active at the same time.

### 6. You are not behind, you are overloaded
Hook:
Sometimes the feeling of being behind is really the feeling of carrying too many open loops.

### 7. The hidden tax of too many priorities
Hook:
Every extra active priority taxes your attention more than you think.

## Pillar 2. Better decision systems

### 8. Stop asking what you feel like doing
Hook:
If you choose work based on mood, your priorities will drift constantly.

### 9. The real question is not what is good
Hook:
The real question is not what ideas are good. It is which one deserves focus now.

### 10. What to kill, pause, or pursue
Hook:
A better system asks three questions: what do I pursue, what do I pause, and what do I kill?

### 11. Why prioritization feels hard
Hook:
Prioritization feels hard when your criteria are vague.

### 12. Clear criteria beat inspiration
Hook:
Good decisions usually come more from clear criteria than from inspiration.

### 13. Your priorities are too emotional
Hook:
If your priorities change every day, they may be running on emotion instead of a real decision system.

### 14. Opportunity ranking is underrated
Hook:
Most people do not need more ideas. They need a better way to rank the ones they already have.

## Pillar 3. Clearer execution and operating visibility

### 15. Poor visibility creates fake confusion
Hook:
A lot of confusion is not real complexity. It is poor visibility.

### 16. If you cannot see it, you cannot manage it
Hook:
If you cannot see what is active, what is paused, and what is next, execution gets sloppy fast.

### 17. Most people do not need a bigger system
Hook:
Most people do not need a more complicated system. They need a clearer operating view.

### 18. The weekly review is not optional
Hook:
If you never review your priorities, your system will quietly rot.

### 19. Your next step should be obvious
Hook:
A good system makes the next step painfully obvious.

### 20. Visibility reduces stress
Hook:
Sometimes stress does not go down when work decreases. It goes down when visibility improves.

## Selection guidance

Start with the simplest, strongest hooks:
- You do not have a motivation problem
- Too many ideas is not always a strength
- The real question is not what is good
- Poor visibility creates fake confusion
- Visibility reduces stress
