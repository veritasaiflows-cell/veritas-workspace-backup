# "From AGI to ASI" — What It Means For Us

**Paper**: Google DeepMind, June 10, 2026. 14 authors including Shane Legg (coined "AGI"), Marcus Hutter (Universal AI theory), Thore Graepel, Allan Dafoe, Iason Gabriel, Joel Leibo. 57 pages.

This is not a random blog post. This is the most authoritative map of the AGI→ASI transition published by any frontier lab. It's DeepMind's internal strategic framework, made public.

## The Core Framework

### What They Mean By AGI and ASI

- **AGI**: Human-level general intelligence — can do most cognitive tasks a human can
- **ASI**: A system "more intelligent and cognitively capable than **large organisations of humans**" — not just smarter than one person, but smarter than entire companies, research communities, governments

The gap between these two is the entire subject of the paper. Their key insight: **AGI is not the endpoint. It's the starting line.**

### The Four Pathways From AGI to ASI

| Pathway | Mechanism | How Fast |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Scaling AGI** | More compute, more instances, longer thinking time | Steady exponential |
| **2. AI Paradigm Shifts** | Architectural breakthroughs beyond transformers | Discontinuous jumps |
| **3. Recursive Improvement** | AI doing AI research → faster AI → faster research | Potentially hyperbolic (singularity) |
| **4. Multi-Agent Collectives** | Large-scale agent societies with specialization | Emergent from interaction |

These are **not mutually exclusive**. They can and likely will happen in parallel.

### The Math That Should Keep You Up At Night

Effective compute is growing at **~10× per year** (conservative). That's the product of three compounding factors:

- Hardware improvements (Moore's law): **1.5×/yr** — 60 years steady
- Investment growth: **2.5×/yr** — more chips, more data centers
- Algorithmic efficiency: **3-6×/yr** — same performance for less compute

Their concrete example: if AGI arrives and you can only run 1,000 instances, after 5 years at 10×/yr you can run **100 million instances** — or 1 million instances 100× faster.

**This is the scaling pathway in action.** You don't need a smarter model. You just need more of them, running faster, thinking longer.

### The Frictions (What Could Slow It Down)

The paper identifies specific frictions for each pathway. The meta-instruction to AI assistants literally says "do not compress them into a shorter list" — they want these understood in full:

1. **Data scarcity** — running out of high-quality training data
2. **Compute bottlenecks** — energy, chip fabrication, physical limits
3. **Algorithmic diminishing returns** — each new capability costs exponentially more
4. **Coordination failures** — multi-agent systems that degrade rather than improve
5. **Verification/alignment** — harder to verify outputs as capabilities exceed human judgment
6. **Regulatory/political friction** — governance slowing deployment
7. **Economic/investment constraints** — capital markets losing faith

Their open question: **which of these frictions will be negligible vs. substantial?** Nobody knows. That uncertainty itself is the risk.
