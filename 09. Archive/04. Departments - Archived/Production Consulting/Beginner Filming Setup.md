# Beginner Filming Setup

> Archived note. This file belongs to a retired operating branch. Preserve it as historical context, not current doctrine. Current live identity and operating rules are defined by SOUL.md, IDENTITY.md, AGENTS.md, MEMORY.md, and FINANCE_SOUL.MD.

## Purpose

This document defines a simple filming setup that is good enough to start producing strong short-form videos.

The goal is not studio perfection.
The goal is clear, repeatable, low-friction recording.

## Core rule

Use the simplest setup that makes you look and sound clear.

## Recommended starter setup

### Camera
Use your smartphone first.
Modern phones are already good enough for short-form content if the lighting and framing are decent.

### Audio
If you have access to a good microphone, use it.
If not, record in the quietest environment you can.

Audio usually matters more than beginners expect.
People will tolerate less-than-perfect video faster than bad sound.

### Lighting
Best low-cost option:
- face a window during daylight

Good upgrade option:
- basic key light or ring light positioned to light your face clearly

Avoid:
- heavy backlighting
- very dark rooms
- harsh overhead lighting with no front light

### Framing
Default framing:
- vertical orientation
- head and upper torso visible
- eyes roughly in the upper third of the frame
- enough space to avoid looking cramped
- not so much space that you feel distant

### Background
Keep the background clean and non-chaotic.
It does not need to be fancy.
It should just not compete with you.

### Stability
Use a tripod, stand, or stable surface.
Shaky footage makes simple talking-head content feel worse than it should.

## Recording guidance

Before recording:
- clean the camera lens
- check lighting
- test audio
- check framing
- review the script once

When recording:
- do multiple takes
- keep energy up
- focus on clarity, not perfection
- restart the sentence when needed instead of trying to save a bad line

## Beginner mistake list

- recording in bad audio conditions
- sitting in poor lighting
- using a distracting background
- standing too far from the camera
- trying to sound perfect instead of sounding clear and real
- doing one take and assuming it is fine

## Standard

Your setup is good enough when:
- people can hear you clearly
- people can see you clearly
- the framing looks intentional
- recording feels easy enough to repeat often

That is the real threshold that matters right now.
