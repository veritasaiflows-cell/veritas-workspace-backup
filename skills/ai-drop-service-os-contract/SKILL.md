# AI Drop-Service Contract

## Procedure

1. Classify the business model as physical product dropshipping, AI drop servicing, automation agency, or workflow productization. Compare three niches and disqualifiers; return pursue, defer, or reject. Prefer a narrow service-as-system offer over physical supply-chain operations when local workflow audits, research, templates, and proof-backed delivery fit the buyer.

2. Establish the authority contract. Scope research, public source-labeled lead preparation, fictional/anonymized demos, draft offers, templates, validators, and leased internal helper outputs separately from owner-gated actions. Require exact approval for persistent-agent creation, account/channel access, outreach, publishing, customer delivery, client-system access, payments, spend, installs, OAuth, credentials, runtime/config/network/service changes, private customer data, or legal, tax, compliance, guarantee, income, ROI, or performance claims. Exclude deceptive advertising, fake identities/testimonials/case studies, private-data scraping, spam, infringement, and unsupported claims. Keep finance/account/capital/execution outside existing finance guardrails blocked. Finish with exact allowed writes and stop lines; internal readiness grants no external authority.

3. Design one offer for one buyer, problem, and measurable deliverable. Start with an AI Workflow Clarity Sprint when suitable: workflow map, automation opportunities, tool-stack recommendation, risk list, implementation plan, and proof-backed demo; add a small prototype only after diagnostic validation. Draft scope, price test, timeline, intake, revision/support limits, proof checklist, and guarantee-safe language. Avoid generic agency positioning, broad automation promises, guaranteed revenue, and customer-facing finance products without separate gates. Verify every material claim against a source-backed claims register.

4. Prepare continuity before helper dispatch or multi-hour work. Record workflow/workstream, original request, latest intent, objective, authority, non-goals, stop lines, front-door proof, sources, write mode, leased paths, helper lanes, model route, acceptance, recovery state, last completed step, next safe step, open questions, proof artifacts, and closeout requirement. Run the applicable long-work packet linter at preflight, spawn, and closeout. Check the lane register before writes; finish with a resumable packet under `tmp/` or the approved workflow continuity path.

5. Verify the configured roster using `openclaw agents list --json`; OpenClaw does not discover agents from workspace folders. Assign public research and offer drafting to Research Scout, delivery code/templates to Implementation Builder, claim and implementation challenge to QA Red-Team, and accepted continuity updates to Docs Continuity Editor. Main owns strategy, routing, integration, verification, acceptance, and user-facing judgment. Resolve exact live models and transport through `veritas-model-routing-helper-lanes` and `veritas-isolated-agent-contract`; logical departments are assignments, not reasons to create agents. Verify bootstrap, transport, model parity, memory boundaries, and message routing before dispatch.

6. Freeze a bounded helper contract with role, objective, allowed sources, source-labeled deliverable, exact leased write target, acceptance proof, and stop lines. Use Main-mediated communication by default; use visible session identities only through available transport. Research cannot contact prospects or make final recommendations; Builder cannot mutate credentials, vendors, customers, or runtime; QA challenges hype, privacy, delivery gaps, and over-promising. Verify source/proof, timestamp, confidence, and live artifacts before accepting output. Keep research/QA read-only or distinct-output and Builder writes within proven scoped writeback.

7. Maintain hub-and-spoke continuity: Main controls the shared project board, specialists keep role-local discoveries, and Main promotes accepted facts into canonical project continuity and daily memory. Treat helper memory as unverified and the board as routing state. Record accepted, rejected, and unverified outputs, active/closed lanes, source freshness, proof status, and exact next command or decision after material progress. On recovery, read `SOUL.md`, `USER.md`, `AGENTS.md` and its route map, Startup Truth Index, today/yesterday memory, and the work packet; reconstruct scope, check lanes, verify live artifacts, and resume the next safe step. Stop for a material disagreement between the request and packet.

8. Build the delivery OS: intake, audit, automation map, caveated ROI estimates, implementation backlog, QA checklist, closeout templates, and helper contracts. Run a fictional/anonymized brief through the complete workflow. Resolve or explicitly accept QA findings; finish with a sample deliverable and end-to-end dry-run proof.

9. When outreach email readiness is requested, follow the channel branch below. For private pilots, prepare customer-data rules, onboarding, deliverables, revisions, support, risks, and exact approval boundaries before contact or payment collection. After approved pilot actions, retain minimal action logs and case-study-safe internal proof. Productize only repeated delivery patterns supported by demand and reliability evidence.

10. Propose additional persistent agents or schedules only when needed. A new agent needs repeated evidence of an unmet capability/isolation/memory/tool-policy/routing need plus explicit config approval; specify workspace, state directory, role identity, memory, shared read-only team context, least privilege, routing smoke test, and rollback/delete plan. Stage automation from observe, route, review-only refresh, helper-contract preparation, and guarded proof execution to separately approved owner-gated action. Suggested checks cover board freshness, continuity, weekly research, and overdue-task reporting; initially report overdue work without silently spawning helpers or patching state. Verify no job creates/deletes agents, edits schedules/runtime, sends externally, infers approval, or acts on credentials/customer/finance/account surfaces.

11. Close with chosen model and rejected alternatives, one-sentence offer, buyer, workflow map, templates, helper contracts, claims register, privacy boundary, recovery packet, dry-run proof, QA disposition, and remaining owner-gated actions. When agent creation was authorized, include roster, exact command packet, routing smoke test, and rollback proof. Report build status, monetization/operational risks, what changed, validation limits, and the next action as internal-safe, owner-gated, or blocked. A clean demo or packet proves neither launch approval nor revenue, compliance, or customer readiness.

## Outreach Email Channel Branch

1. Classify the email identity as a channel asset, not access or sending authority. Route SMB Workflow Clarity, Lead Rescue, Marketing Ops, and Website-To-Lead Audit to WF79-SMB; use WF75 for service packaging, training, templates, QA, and private-pilot design. WF74/WF88 support internal proof and continuity only. Finish with the owning workflow and channel boundary recorded.

2. Prepare sender-profile guidance, signature, labels, manual-send SOP, copy, reply scripts, tracker, and approval cards using source-labeled public business information without private/personal enrichment. Specify exact batch, channel, copy, follow-up rule, and data rule. Owner-set copy voice, standing requirement after two rejected draft sets: write like a busy owner typing a quick note to another owner — short sentences, plain words, one specific observation from the prospect's own public material, one honest question, one low-pressure ask, and an easy out. No marketing polish, parallel constructions, punchlines, copywriting formulas, or jargon; a structure that violates the voice gets rejected no matter how correct its offer logic is. Keep personal-email harvesting, automation, customer data, and payment links outside the manual-prep scope; verify the packet leaves sending to the owner.

3. Have the owner sign in and send manually after exact approval. Never request or store passwords, recovery/2FA codes, OAuth tokens, app passwords, cookies, or secret screenshots in chat, memory, files, or packets. Never infer send authority from an email account, draft copy, QA-clean packet, prospect list, pilot-readiness status, or owner enthusiasm. Require separate approval before sign-in delegation, connectors, API access, forwarding, filters, aliases, DNS, CRM/campaign tools, account/security settings, contact-form submissions, replies, follow-ups, bulk sends, warmup, ads, or private-data processing. Classify replies only from approved non-sensitive snippets; finish with recommendation drafts, not unapproved contact.

4. After manual proof, scope an official least-privilege OAuth/delegated connector with audit logging and draft/review-first posture only if requested. Keep send-capable automation gated on repeated manual proof, QA, explicit approval, and a narrow audit trail. Close by reporting whether credentials stayed untouched, messages were sent, connector/runtime/account settings changed, and which exact next action remains gated.
