# Automation Recovery Proof

Use for automation failures, a fleet-status inventory question, expired leases, status-only lane transitions, partial command completion, a recurring guard/keeper job whose reported success may not prove it acted, a hash-pinning guard blocked by an archived artifact, an alert reporting a status-check exit that may be a correct negative, or a config/scheduler change that is a hypothesis needing live verification and rollback rather than a known fix. This branch inspects and verifies; apply repairs only through the owning implementation or automation gate.

1. For automation failures or a fleet-status inventory question, capture the live automation inventory's scope, total, job identities, enabled state, and last-run errors. Separate enabled failures, disabled non-retired jobs, and retired history. If resumed tools become caller-scoped or an earlier job becomes inaccessible, report the visibility limit and require a fresh authenticated Control UI owner turn for Gateway-wide management; do not infer deletion or use shell access to bypass the restriction. A job id returned by the inventory can still fail to resolve in a per-job run-history call (`Automation not found`), so do not retry that id. Read the job's last status, consecutive-error count and error text from the fresh cron control packet (`live_scheduler_last_run_exceptions`, plus the `live_scheduler_*` fields on each freshness signal) and name the substitution, treating the packet as visibility only; step 3's run-history check still decides whether a repair landed. The packet carries its own reconciliation: artifact freshness governs escalation while the scheduler's last status stays separately visible, so report both rather than collapsing them. Escalation therefore follows the artifact classification, not the last-run status alone: a blocked required artifact escalates even when the scheduler reported `ok`, repeated scheduler failures are their own escalation reason, and a single last-run error whose required artifact is healthy is visible scheduler state rather than an incident.

2. When using a saved-snapshot reproduction, check its timestamp, schema, job coverage, and identities against the intended failure before accepting it. Reject an obsolete fixture that produces missing-job errors in place of the reported drift. Preserve that mismatch as a proof limitation; a successful local invocation does not make its inputs current.

3. For a failure surfaced through a ranked queue, docket, or scorecard (for example a WF74 fix-now row or a blocked-step KPI), compare the signal's source-artifact timestamp against the most recent repair or re-verification before diagnosing the producer. A signal generated before a repair landed describes the pre-repair world and resurfaces on every re-rank until its source surface is refreshed. Clear the ranking together with the health proof: re-run the producer with its write flag, then regenerate each downstream ranker that consumed the stale surface. A validation-only or read-only re-run proves the step healthy but leaves the stale artifact in place, so the same false item reappears at the next re-rank; do not call the queue clean until the consuming artifacts are regenerated. For scheduled cron jobs, verify the repair against the job's run history (for example `openclaw cron runs <job-id> --json`) rather than a lagging operator-ledger or freshness-spine snapshot: those can carry a pre-repair `last_error` as a live-looking scheduler exception and re-wake a heartbeat escalation. Adjudicate before repairing — run history showing a successful post-repair run means the only work is refreshing the ledger producer, regenerating the consuming ranker (for example the cron control packet), and confirming it now reports zero escalation signals.

   After regenerating a cron control packet that still has blocked or escalated rows, regenerate the WF74 opportunity queue (`python scripts\wf74_improvement_opportunity_queue.py --write --write-md --validate`) before retrying status-card. Status-card fail-closes on `wf74_pickup.missing_cron_migration_repair` when those rows exist and the queue has no visible cron-migration opportunity. A queue older than the new control packet is that miss: it is not a remaining contract gap and does not justify diagnosing the status-card producer or force-running the status-card cron. Local `status_card_freshness_predispatch_prefilter.py --execute --write --validate` is the proof refresh; scheduler last-run and the freshness spine may stay blocked on those last-run artifacts until the next natural slot.

4. For a job whose spine row repeats as `blocked` with reason `artifact_blocked_or_authority_widened` while the job's own runner reports success (`runner_success=True`), stop diagnosing the runner: read the job contract's `expected_artifacts` list and check each sibling artifact's own `status` and `validation`. One upstream required artifact carrying `critical` (for example a startup-brief packet blocked on a stale finance-digest proof) re-classifies the job's row on every run even though the direct artifact is healthy and fresh. Repair the upstream artifact's producer through its owner route (for a finance proof block: the session-appropriate alerts-chain refresh), then regenerate the consuming control packet and confirm the escalation count is zero. Do not patch the spine classifier: it is correctly reporting the sibling artifact.

5. For an expired lease, inspect its exact write scope and the owning session's terminal result before reconciliation. When work is unfinished and unaccepted, record blocked rather than complete, preserving scope and proof. Expiry alone grants neither acceptance nor permission to take over its files. Verify fresh exact-write admission before applying a separate repair. If the lease is expired with nothing to take over, close it in the lane register first (`--complete` with a note and proof pointer); an expired leased lane keeps being selected as the resume cursor until it is closed, which is what makes the stale checkpoint resurface.

   After the register no longer lists that lease as active, do not refresh `tmp/current-resume.json` with `session_resume_checkpoint.py --write` alone. That path can reuse the existing explicit checkpoint, stamp a new `generated_at_utc`, and keep the closed lane's `exact_next_command`. Record a replacement cursor with `--record` (objective, blocked or in-progress status, and a read-only next command) or derive the empty pointer when no active lane remains. Treat the artifact as current only after `lane_id`, `exact_next_command`, `freshness_expiry`, and `command_authorized` match live register state—not `generated_at`.

6. For a status-only lane transition, preserve its existing parent job, lane, phase and attempt/retry identity. Use `--set-status` with `--status-value` and notes/proof; omit a replacement `--phase` merely describing the blocker. If attempt correlation rejects such a command, remove the incidental identity change rather than invent a new attempt.

   After a lane-manager timeout or nonzero exit, inspect the durable register and `post_write_refresh` separately. A status transition may persist before attribution refresh fails. Confirm the process has ended, identify untouched transitions, and resume only those; do not replay the entire batch. Inspect the per-transition refresh time allowance when budgeting a sequential batch. Verify register status and identity, validation, and attribution refresh independently at closeout.

7. For a missing retired bootstrap input, trace both the prefilter's required-input list and downstream consumers to the current owner. Local tool notes belong to `AGENTS.md`'s Tools section, not a required `TOOLS.md`. Repair the obsolete dependency within the approved source scope rather than recreate a retired authority file. Add regressions for both absent retired input and absent active required input; confirm the invoked test entry point actually runs them. Keep any untouched downstream consumer explicitly unresolved.

8. Accept only what the final proof covers: applied-source hashes, focused tests, lease disposition, and metadata refresh are separate from scheduler execution. A disabled job stays disabled until its exact gate is approved; local tests or later producer proof cannot clear historical job status. Report remaining contract drift, intentional containment, and inaccessible fleet state without declaring a green fleet.

9. For improvement-ledger closure work, decompose `latest_open_count` before treating it as a classification backlog. Each open row is exactly one of: standing monitor-only (`follow_up.status: monitor_only_standing`, kept visible by design, not debt), a live candidate (no `follow_up` block — the source packet still emits the signal), or follow-up debt (`follow_up.required: true`, as `missing_followup` or `manual_review_required`). Only the third class is classification work; the open total alone overstates it. Classify by refreshing the producers the triage rules read, rerunning `wf88_followup_debt_triage_packet.py --write --validate`, then `improvement_ledger.py --write --validate`.

   A triage `closure_allowed: true` does not guarantee the ledger appends the closure. The closure event fires only when the ledger's latest row for that source key is itself a follow-up-required row. When the source re-emits the signal after an earlier closure, the newest row is a fresh candidate carrying no `follow_up`, so `--write` appends 0 and the row stays open — correct fail-closed behavior, not a failed apply. The row closes when the source stops emitting it, or when it converts to follow-up debt and triage re-allows. Trace the per-key row history (`decision`, `status`, `follow_up.required` over time) before concluding the closure path is broken. Rows parked at `manual_review_required` matched no deterministic triage rule; they clear only through a new rule or an owner packet, so do not re-run the refresh loop expecting them to move.

10. When a P1 or repeated job failure arrives during a frozen observation window (a declared multi-session gate with voiding conditions, like G8), adjudicate window impact before diagnosing the failure itself. Three checks, in order: (a) compare the failing job against the window's declared grid — a runtime or proof job outside the declared grid has no window impact regardless of its error count; (b) compare failure timestamps against the observation hours (for G8, 06:00-13:00 Phoenix) — failures outside those hours do not void a session; (c) when Main made same-day edits to surfaces the job consumes, order the timeline before ruling them in or out — a first failure predating the edits excludes them as the trigger, and later passing runs with the edits still in place completes the exclusion. Record the adjudication in the window declaration's addendum with each voiding condition checked and not fired, and state what the root cause is not without claiming the root cause is established. A self-healed job (later runs pass without intervention) still needs its escalation signal cleared: rerun the consuming packet and confirm escalation reaches zero; the scheduler's error-count ledger can lag run history, and run history is the healing oracle.

11. When a fail-closed job exits red on a state that is itself truthful, classify the red as the design working, not as a defect, before touching any repair. After steps 3-4 locate the producing artifact, read that artifact's validation errors and map them to their producer semantics; then check whether the state they describe is real. Four verified shapes: (a) a control dispatcher whose consumed packet carries `status=blocked` because the packet truthfully reports genuine blocked domain lanes — repair belongs to the lane owners or an owner decision, never to the dispatcher or packet generator, and forcing the packet green would create the false-green the fail-closed design exists to prevent; (b) a weekly alerts chain run inside a closed-market window whose controller reports `freshness_decay` on last-completed-session quotes — validation flags such as `alert_level_freshness` and `recurring_run_completed_with_visible_debt` are the expected domain state, clear on the next market-session chain, and must not be forced green out of slot; (c) the same closed-market window where the red is instead a **structural mis-grade** — a consumer grading a closed-market window against a live-intraday age ceiling. Separate (c) from (b) by the producer's own verdict, never by the consumer's label: a mis-grade carries a clean producer quote validation (`status=ok`, `critical_count=0`, warnings only), a calendar status marking the quote current for the last completed session, and the window's closed-market tolerance flag stamped true — while the consumer overrides that with an intraday age test. Verified 2026-09-21: the weekday 06:05 pre-open morning chain scored 25 of 32 rows `freshness_decay` on Friday-close quotes at 65.09h against a 36h weekday ceiling, producer validation clean, every decay row carrying the identical reason pair; replaying the same retained artifacts at the weekend tolerance scored 0 decay with 32 monitor-only. (d) an alert that a status-check command exited nonzero — for example a closure check exiting 1 because the gate it tests is genuinely still open — where the alert wording ("failed") mislabels a correct negative as a fault: read the state the check tests, and when the checked condition is genuinely false the nonzero exit is the check working, expected state rather than an incident, clearing at the next evaluation after the condition becomes true (verified 2026-09-23: a heartbeat reported a command failing exit 1 while checking whether G8 had been marked closed; the register showed 4 of 5 sessions banked with the final session in progress, so no repair was owed). A real domain state keeps its honest warning; only a technical defect earns a repair.

    Two guards before proposing that repair. First, isolate the single driver rather than assuming it: check every other input against its own ceiling (that run's reference-level age was 115h against a 336h ceiling, so quote age was the sole cause), and do the structural arithmetic, because a window whose run time always falls after a closed-market gap exceeds an intraday ceiling **by construction** on the first session after any gap — that pre-open run sits 65h after a Friday close and 17h after a Monday close. Second, prove the widened gate does not mask the adjacent real positive: replay retained artifacts for both the repaired case and a genuine live-hours decay case, and require the live-hours case to still decay (here the same run's intermittent `market_hours_fresh` `stale_unexpected` flag on a 17-minute-old quote had to survive the widening). The repair belongs to the window-class contract — grade a closed-market window by the producer's calendar verdict and keep the intraday ceiling for live-session windows. When a recorded owner decision pins the current value, do not apply the repair and do not argue its merits: stage the exact hunks, regression tests, validation plan and rollback, and validate the patch against retained artifacts on a scratch copy, so the owner's decision arrives as a yes/no. The implementation owner's owner-decision pre-check governs that hold.

12. When a proof gate or test fails after your own edits, attribute the failure to the change or to pre-existing state by controlled A/B before diagnosing either. Stash exactly the files you changed (`git stash push -m <label> -- <files>`), re-run the failing gate read-only (no `--write`, no artifact mutation), and compare status, error count, and error text. An identical failure on HEAD code is pre-existing: pop the stash, confirm `git diff --numstat` reproduces exactly your intended change, and report the failure as out-of-scope evidence rather than a regression. A different or vanished failure puts your diff back under suspicion. Verified twice on 2026-09-20: a router test failing on missing live `tmp/deployment-readiness-surface.json` (absent because full window chains had not run since the retired-state quarantine) and the pivot validator's two alerts-chain proof errors each reproduced identically on HEAD, closing two would-be misattributions without touching either producer. Step 10's timeline comparison is the passive variant of this rule; when the working tree is ahead of HEAD and the suspected delta is constant values rather than files, replay the pre-edit values instead (the routing owner's policy-reconciliation procedure).

13. When a hash-validated freshness consumer still fails after its producer was already refreshed, check whether the stale files were written by the current repair session itself before diagnosing the producer again. Validation, memory, and packet writes made during diagnosis change source hashes after the producer's rebuild, so a consumer re-run flags them as new staleness. Sequence the work producer-last: finish all diagnostic writes first, then re-run the producer with its write flag, then the consumer. Verified 2026-09-20: the memory-vector-graph scorecard failed on `memory/2026-09-19.md` (already healed by the 06:08 maintenance run), but the re-run still failed on `memory/2026-09-20.md` and `tmp/alerts-os-pivot-validator.json` — both written by that morning's own repair and verification; running `semantic_memory_maintenance.py` with the cron's exact argv after the last write, then the scorecard, returned exit 0 with zero errors. Local script proof alone does not clear scheduler last-run state: a forced canary of the review-only job (no delivery) then reset `consecutiveErrors` 4→0.

14. When a validator flags a value at a threshold (size, count, budget, staleness limit), establish which side owns the flagged value before changing either one. Test whether the value is written by a runtime or platform reconciler from a constant outside this workspace: find the constant in the runtime bundle, then look for a reconciler that projects it into live state. If it is, editing the local copy is not durable — the next reconcile restores it and the edit then registers as drift, converting a warning into a real defect. Correct the threshold instead, deliberately and visibly: a named constant with its reason recorded, scoped to the owning class alone, emitted per item and in the summary, and verified not to have relaxed the sibling class. Budgeting a class apart from the default is not an exemption — a value over the new allowance must still flag. Do not silence the warning, weaken the check globally, or recreate state to clear it. Step 11 classifies a red before any repair — a truthful domain state or a structural mis-grade; this step governs an honest warning whose threshold is scoped wrong, where the threshold — not the state — is the defect. Verified 2026-09-20: `cron_prompt_bloat_present` flagged 10 `skill-collection-review-*` contracts at 3,199 chars against a 1,800 budget, and both `expected` and `live` reported the same count, so it was a contract property rather than live drift. The prompt is the runtime's own skill-collection review prompt, re-projected into reserved `skill-collection-review:` declarations on every reconcile, so trimming was never durable. Budgeting reconciler-projected declarations separately returned the fleet to `ok` with drift 0 and warnings 0, every prompt byte-identical and no schedule or payload mutated.

15. When a recurring guard, keeper or hygiene job reports success, verify that it performed its action before treating it as working: a green `lastRunStatus`, exit code or job status proves the process ran, not that it acted. Read the job's own status artifact for the recorded action (a `skipped` / `rearmed` / `already_resident` field), because a keeper can report `ok` on every run while recording in its own payload that it did nothing. Two causes were verified together on 2026-09-20 for an hourly embedding-residency keeper. (a) It resolved its target from config paths that do not exist in the live config (`agents.defaults.memorySearch`, `providers.ollama.baseUrl` against a config carrying `memory.search` and `models.providers.ollama.baseUrl`), so the target resolved null and the job no-opped indefinitely while reporting `ok`. A resolver that reads a non-existent path returns null rather than failing, so check the resolver's paths against the live config shape, record which source resolved, and accept the historical shape alongside the canonical one so a later rename cannot silently disable the feature again. (b) It armed only when the target was absent, so a short default expiry restored the original penalty after every idle gap even though the target read as resident; a residency or TTL keeper must also re-arm on expiring residency, not only on absence. Prove the fix by exercising the action path the keeper owns rather than re-reading its status: force the absent state, confirm the keeper re-arms and holds, then confirm the scheduled run records that action.

16. When verifying a condition-triggered watcher job, check the design as well as the last run, because a watcher's failure mode is silence. Confirm its stored state carries the observed value it compares against: a job with no recorded baseline can fire on its own creation, so the first evaluation must record state without firing. Confirm it can report its own blindness, since a watcher authored around success sits green while its source is unreadable; a failed observation must eventually raise one explicit warning rather than stay silent, and a fired message must name the transition it saw so a plumbing test cannot be mistaken for a real event. Confirm the message is self-contained, because the trigger message is the fired run's entire context, and confirm an explicit owner is bound: `automations add` warns `No --agent specified` and still creates the job, and the same fleet carried a job failing every run with `AGENT_SELECTION_REQUIRED` (Multiple agents are configured, but this operation has no explicit owner), so bind `--agent` at creation rather than treating the warning as harmless. Verified 2026-09-21 on a release-watch job whose forced test recorded a baseline, declined to imply a release it had not seen, and carried the fail-streak warning; the agent was pinned at creation after that warning appeared. Confirm too that every path the payload reads lives outside `tmp/`: a reaped `tmp/` artifact silently breaks a job whose payload names it, so copy a probe or script the payload depends on to a durable path before the payload references it (verified 2026-09-21: the spawn probe was copied from `tmp/` to `scripts/` for exactly this reason).

    Then check the payload's own premise, because a watcher whose message asserts the state it should be discovering will keep firing true statements about the transition and false instructions about the work. A payload that names a specific item to re-test, chase or re-open encodes external state that changes independently of the watcher; when that item closes or is superseded, the watcher is still correct and its recommended action is now wrong. Bind the payload to re-derive the premise from the same source the trigger reads rather than asserting it, or state the premise as a condition the run must re-check before acting, so a stale payload degrades into a failed check instead of a confidently wrong instruction. When the premise does change, updating the payload is a scheduler mutation with its own gate — report the stale instruction and request the edit rather than letting a future run act on it or editing the schedule as part of a review. Verified 2026-09-22: a release-watch payload instructing a re-test of a named issue was still correct for its own trigger, but that issue had been closed as superseded the previous day, so the run's instruction needed re-derivation and the correction needed separate approval rather than a silent payload edit.

    A trigger-evaluation failure is this class's execution-side sibling: a job failing at ~10ms duration with `AGENT_SELECTION_REQUIRED` (or any trigger-evaluation error) failed before its payload dispatched — no runner step executed — so do not debug the runner; its last artifact can be clean and days old precisely because nothing has run since the fleet gained another agent and the job never carried an explicit owner. Repair by binding one (`openclaw cron edit <id> --agent <agent>`; schedule and payload untouched), then prove it with a forced run executing the full payload. Treat that first full execution as the first real evidence about the runner: the trigger failure masked everything downstream, so expect an unrelated payload defect to surface now (verified 2026-09-22: after binding the agent on a two-day-failing earnings job, the forced run exposed a missing canonical-input artifact the trigger failure had been hiding). The consecutive-error count formally clears only at the next natural scheduled slot; until then the control packet reports recovery as recognized-pending-natural-canary, which step 1's reconciliation governs.

17. When a hash-pinning consumer guard (a lineage or existence check carrying a recorded expected sha) blocks on an artifact that is missing, locate the dated cleanup or archive directory before diagnosing the guard or regenerating the artifact: an authorized tmp-cleanup archives rather than deletes, so the archived copy is usually the authentic original. Restore it and verify the restored file's hash equals the pinned expected value exactly — the pin itself is the provenance proof — then rerun the guard and regenerate the consuming packets. Verified 2026-09-22: the SQL-canon access guard failed `current_lineage_artifacts_exist_and_hash_match` with an expected sha and a null actual for a backlog artifact that 279 lineage rows pin; it had been archived that morning into the dated tmp-cleanup archive, and the restored copy's hash matched the pin exactly, returning the guard to `ok` and the control packet with it. Restoring one file repairs the symptom only: the cleanup's exclusion set still lacks the lineage-pinned live artifacts, so the next cleanup re-breaks the same guard — route the exclusion-list update to the cleanup owner (`veritas-os2-cleanup-router`) as a separate change rather than weakening the guard or re-deleting the artifact.

18. When a scheduler or config change is a hypothesis rather than a known fix, run it as a verification loop with a rollback prepared before the change, and prefer the narrower approval boundary the platform already provides before widening a global one. Sequence: (a) back up the target file first and keep the backup until the loop closes; (b) change exactly one named leaf per attempt, never two, so the result attributes to one cause; (c) verify with a forced live run against the same check that originally failed (for a scheduler job force the next run, for a consumer gate re-run the consuming packet) rather than trusting a dry-run or a config-parse check, because a parse-clean or no-op run proves nothing about the behavior the repair targets; (d) if the forced run still fails the same way, the hypothesis is disproven — roll back byte-identical from the backup (confirm sha256 equality after the restore) and report the failed hypothesis and its evidence, not a repair; (e) only after rollback, read the platform's own documentation for the narrower scoped mechanism instead of retrying a guess. Verified 2026-09-23/24: the `workspaceAccess: ro` hypothesis was disproven by a forced verification run that returned the same mount-rejection, rolled back byte-identical from the pre-change backup (sha256 equality confirmed), and the doc then surfaced the already-existing per-agent `dangerouslyAllowExternalBindSources` override. A forced verification run of the scoped combined change returned `ok` the next day, proving the mechanism. The escalation clears only when the consuming packet is regenerated: `openclaw cron runs <job-id> --json` (or the consumer's `--write --validate` re-run) is the oracle, not the scheduler's own last-run error field, which lags the real recovery.

Two guards. First, a hypothesis that depends on a specific mechanism is unproven until the live run produces the mechanism's observable signature: a mount rejection disappearing, an escalation count reaching zero, or a forced run that now records the intended action. Second, a change applied to satisfy an alert is not approved merely because the alert disappears — the approving surface is the owner or the explicitly scoped gate, and the change's scope is the one named leaf, so a wider change that also happens to clear the alert is still an over-change.

19. When a PM job or workflow item surfaces an earlier repair's live-looking result that still keeps the item open, check the completion-ledger entry's recency before treating the item as done. A job is not complete because an old ledger row exists for its ID — only a completion newer than the latest live-looking problem state closes the lane. Verified 2026-09-24: a priority-96 cron repair stayed listed as live because the completion ledger held a 2026-06-21 row for the same ID, even after the live run had been repaired at a later date. Two repairs, separately verified: the cron regression item and the planning follow-through gap were both restored as open and ranked P1 once the old ledger entry stopped counting as completion. Report each item truthfully as reopened with its own next action, not as a stale artifact.
