# Model Portfolio

## Purpose

Define the target structure for portfolio construction and sizing.

This is the model, not a broker-connected execution system.

## Allocation framework

- Planning capital base: **$10,000 flat**
- Cash reserve: **10% / $1,000** target
- Investable capital: **90% / $9,000**
- Core holdings: 50% to 60%
- Tactical positions: 25% to 35%
- Speculative sleeve: 10% to 15%

## Position sizing tiers

Percent rules remain account-level. Dollar math below assumes the approved **$10,000** capital base and 10% cash reserve.

- Tier 1, high conviction: 8% to 12% normally = **$800-$1,200 target**, usually staged as **$400-$600 initial tranche** plus a second validated add
- Tier 2, moderate conviction: 4% to 7% = **$400-$700 target**, usually staged as **$200-$350 initial tranche** plus a second validated add
- Tier 3, speculative or asymmetric: 1% to 3% = **$100-$300 target**, usually staged as **$50-$150 initial tranche** plus a second validated add

## Concentration limits

- Normal max single position: 15%
- Stretch max single position: 20% to 25% only for exceptional high-conviction cases and only with explicit review
- Max single sector: 25%
- Use caution before exceeding normal cash deployment during unstable macro conditions

## Portfolio design rules

- Every new position should have a thesis, risk envelope, catalyst path, and invalidation point
- Do not confuse conviction with concentration
- Rebalance to maintain target exposures and reduce drift
- Consider tax efficiency when relevant
- Avoid forcing full deployment just because cash exists
- Use **fractional-share and two-tranche planning** as the default. Whole-share-only implementation is allowed only when it stays inside the intended tier and concentration rules.
- ETFs are valid active-deployment candidates when they solve a portfolio-construction problem better than a single name, especially for diversification, sector exposure, or high-price-name sizing constraints.

## Open decisions

- Randall's preferred risk tolerance: balanced, but comfortable with medium-high volatility if thesis is intact
- Randall's target time horizon: multi-horizon, spanning days, weeks, months, and years with long-term wealth as the dominant objective
- Target benchmark or comparison set: broad market outperformance is desirable, but absolute wealth building also matters
- Tax sensitivity assumptions: decide later
