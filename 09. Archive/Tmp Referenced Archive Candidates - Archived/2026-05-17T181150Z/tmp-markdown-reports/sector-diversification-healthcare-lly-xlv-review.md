## Business quality

LLY qualifies as high-quality growth, not just defensive Healthcare exposure.

- **Growth engine:** Lilly reported Q1 2026 revenue of **$19.8B**, up **56% YoY**, driven primarily by volume growth, with Mounjaro and Zepbound leading Key Products growth.
- **Earnings power:** Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS was **$8.55**, up **156% YoY**. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to **$82B–$85B revenue** and **$35.50–$37.00 non-GAAP EPS**.
- **Franchise depth:** Mounjaro revenue was **$8.7B** in Q1 2026, up **125% YoY**; Zepbound U.S. revenue was **$4.1B**, up **79% YoY**. The diabetes / obesity franchise remains the core of the case.
- **Pipeline / optionality:** Lilly highlighted FDA approval of oral GLP-1 Foundayo/orforglipron for obesity and positive Phase 3 updates across Foundayo, Jaypirca, Taltz/Zepbound combination, and retatrutide.
- **Quality caveat:** This is still a concentrated franchise story. The same obesity / incretin exposure that creates the growth premium also creates vulnerability to reimbursement pressure, realized-price declines, manufacturing bottlenecks, safety headlines, and competitive GLP-1 dynamics.

## Risks/counterarguments

- **Valuation risk:** At roughly 27x–28x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance using the workspace close, Lilly still prices in strong execution. A good business can still be a poor entry if the market is already capitalizing the upside.
- **Product concentration:** Mounjaro and Zepbound are doing most of the heavy lifting. Franchise concentration is acceptable only while demand, access, supply, safety, and pricing remain supportive.
- **Realized price pressure:** Lilly's Q1 release explicitly noted lower realized prices for Mounjaro and Zepbound as offsets to volume growth.
- **Sector tape lag:** XLV is not showing leadership. Buying a great company inside a lagging sector can work, but it reduces margin for error if the individual chart is also extended.
- **Pipeline execution risk:** Oral GLP-1 / retatrutide optionality is valuable, but late-stage success, approval, adoption, and reimbursement are not guaranteed.
- **Policy/reimbursement risk:** Obesity and diabetes economics remain exposed to payer behavior, government pricing, employer-plan coverage, and international reimbursement pressure.
