# Video Quality Standards

> Archived note. This file belongs to a retired operating branch. Preserve it as historical context, not current doctrine. Current live identity and operating rules are defined by SOUL.md, IDENTITY.md, AGENTS.md, MEMORY.md, and FINANCE_SOUL.MD.

## Purpose

This document defines what “good” should mean for Randall’s short-form videos.

The standard is not cinematic perfection.
The standard is clear, engaging, trustworthy, and competitive enough to hold attention.

## Core standard

A strong short-form video should be:
- clear
- easy to hear
- easy to follow
- visually clean
- paced well
- worth watching to the end

## Quality checklist

### 1. Hook quality
The first 1 to 2 seconds should make the viewer care.

Good hooks usually do one of these:
- name a painful truth
- challenge a bad assumption
- create relevant curiosity
- promise a useful insight
- make the viewer feel seen

If the opening is weak, the rest of the video barely matters.

### 2. Audio quality
Audio must be clear enough that people do not strain to understand you.

Minimum standard:
- voice is easy to hear
- background noise is controlled
- volume is consistent
- echo is not distracting

Bad audio makes the whole video feel low quality, even when the image looks fine.

### 3. Visual quality
The image does not need to be fancy.
It does need to be clean.

Minimum standard:
- face is visible
- framing is intentional
- lighting is good enough to avoid looking dull or muddy
- background is not chaotic or distracting
- camera is stable

### 4. Delivery quality
Delivery should feel direct, alive, and believable.

Minimum standard:
- speak clearly
- do not sound half-asleep
- do not rush so much that clarity drops
- do not drag so much that retention dies
- sound like a human with conviction, not like you are reading a memo

### 5. Structural quality
A strong short-form video should move cleanly.

Default structure:
1. hook
2. problem or tension
3. insight or reframe
4. practical takeaway
5. light CTA if useful

### 6. Caption quality
Captions should help retention, not clutter the screen.

Minimum standard:
- easy to read
- timed correctly
- not covering key visual areas
- clean enough to support understanding

### 7. Pacing quality
Every sentence should earn its place.

If a line does not add tension, clarity, or value, it should probably go.

## What low quality usually looks like

- weak or generic opening
- poor audio
- rambling delivery
- too much dead space
- cluttered captions
- no real point
- too many ideas in one video
- trying to sound smart instead of trying to be clear

## Real standard for the first phase

In the beginning, the target is not perfect polish.
The target is to consistently produce videos that are clear, credible, and improving.

That is enough to start learning fast.
